## A. Instructor rubric (for the page)

### Rubric for the Final Project

One hundred points in ten parts of ten: one for the proposal meeting, four for the presentation,
five for the write-up. The bullets say what each part is worth, so you know exactly what to
submit. The write-up is graded like a lab report, because it is one: a lab assignment, modeled on
[Lab 4 — Cell Phone Tower Placement](lab-04/README.md), that a future student could do.

#### Proposal meeting (10)

| Item | Points |
| --- | --- |
| **Proposal meeting** (Week 14, by Friday 5:00 pm)<br>• Met with the instructor as a team before the deadline, and recorded your team, idea and data on the class Google document (2)<br>• The problem in one sentence, and why it matters to someone (2)<br>• The data-feasibility table, one row per layer — source and URL, vintage, format, coordinate system, raster or vector, license — with every cell filled (3)<br>• A sketch of the model, with the raster and vector inputs and the tools new to the course marked (2)<br>• Division of labor, if you are a team of two, and a schedule against the milestones (1) | /10 |

#### Presentation (40)

| Item | Points |
| --- | --- |
| **The question and the data**<br>• The question in one sentence, stated in the first minute, and who would act on the answer (3)<br>• The study area shown, and the coordinate system named (2)<br>• Every layer with its source and vintage, and which are raster and which are vector (3)<br>• The criteria and thresholds the model applies, each with its reason (2) | /10 |
| **The model and the workflow**<br>• The whole model shown, exported from ModelBuilder and readable in the video (3)<br>• The workflow walked tool by tool, in the order it runs, so a classmate could follow it (3)<br>• The tools new to the course named, with what each one does (2)<br>• One decision you had to make: what the alternatives were, and why you chose as you did (2) | /10 |
| **The result and the map**<br>• Lead with the map: the result answers the question in five seconds (3)<br>• Title, legend, scale bar, north arrow and sources on the map, readable at video resolution (2)<br>• The answer stated with numbers, not only shown (2)<br>• One validation or sensitivity check, and what it changed (2)<br>• What the analysis cannot tell you (1) | /10 |
| **Delivery**<br>• The link posted on the class Google document by Wednesday 11:59 pm of Week 15; the video plays, with clear audio, and runs about eight minutes (3)<br>• Every team member speaks, and each can explain the model (2)<br>• Slides legible: large type, one idea per slide, the map and the model shown large (2)<br>• The story holds from question to recommendation and holds a classmate's attention — the interest, cleverness and fun factor your classmates score on their scoresheets (3) | /10 |
| **Subtotal — presentation** | **/40** |

#### Lab-style write-up (50)

| Item | Points |
| --- | --- |
| **Background, Problem Statement and Considerations**<br>• Title block: project title, team members, date and course; and your three notes — your share of the work as a percentage, the hours you spent, and whether a future student should try this project (1)<br>• Background: why the question matters and to whom, in your own words, with sources (2)<br>• Problem Statement: the question in one sentence, the study area, and the criteria and thresholds the model applies (2)<br>• Considerations: the coordinate system and cell size, chosen on purpose and said why; each criterion justified by a source, a standard or an argument (3)<br>• Deliverables and References sections written for the future student who does this lab (1)<br>• Organized writing: figures numbered and referred to in the text, sources credited, the AI line, this rubric pasted with your self-assessment; "utilize" and "in order to" nowhere in it (1) | /10 |
| **Data**<br>• Every layer documented — source and URL, vintage, format, coordinate system, raster or vector, license — the feasibility table from your proposal, completed (4)<br>• Raster and vector data both used in the analysis, not only drawn under it (2)<br>• What each layer can and cannot answer — resolution, vintage, gaps — and what you did about it (2)<br>• The data reachable by a future student: a hosted package or working links, with a note on what to download and where to put it (2) | /10 |
| **ModelBuilder model** — correct and working<br>• The model reruns from raw inputs through its tool dialog without hand repair, and your toolbox (`.atbx`) is uploaded with the report (4)<br>• A full-page figure of the model exported from ModelBuilder, every tool and dataset readable, labels informative (2)<br>• A screen capture of the tool dialog with the inputs, outputs and key thresholds exposed as parameters, and the tool's metadata filled in (2)<br>• The tools new to the course named, with what each does and why it was the right one (2) | /10 |
| **Step-by-Step Solution and Results**<br>• Numbered steps with short titles, each naming the tool, its settings and a figure, so a future student could rebuild the model from them (3)<br>• Check values: the counts, areas or ranges a future student should see at the steps where a mistake would show (1)<br>• The result: the answer to your question, with the numbers that support it (2)<br>• One validation or sensitivity check — change a threshold and show what moves, or compare the result with something independent — and what it changed (2)<br>• Limitations: what the analysis cannot tell you, where the result is wrong, and what data would fix it (2) | /10 |
| **Map** (full page)<br>• Title, neat line, north arrow and scale bar (1)<br>• Text box with authors, date, map projection, and the sources and dates of the data (1)<br>• The result clearly symbolized, with a legend, so the map answers the question at a glance (2)<br>• The inputs that explain the result — boundary, labeled places, the layers the criteria came from (2)<br>• An inset or close-up of the place that matters most (2)<br>• Basemap, scale and legibility appropriate to the study area (2) | /10 |
| **Total** | **/50** |

Proposal meeting 10 + presentation 40 + write-up 50 = **100 points**.

### Assumptions I made

- **Peer scores are feedback, not grade.** `grading.md` puts all 40 presentation points on the
  instructor and gives the watcher 10 points for notes; nothing in the policy feeds the peer average
  into the presenting team's grade. The draft follows the policy. If you want the peer average to
  count, the cleanest swap is to make the **Delivery** row the peer-scored one (its last bullet
  already names the classmates' "fun factor" category): instructor 30 + class average scaled to 10.
- **Presentation-notes points are 5 + 5**, scores and notes, rather than one point per video. The
  page's wording ("turn in what you learned that was new or interesting") is about the notes, so
  the notes carry half. One point per video is the obvious alternative and is a one-line change.
- **The write-up is one document per team**, and each member submits the same document with their
  own three notes (percentage, hours, recommend). The rubric's title-block bullet is written that
  way. If each member writes separately, the Data and Model rows will score identically anyway.
- **"About eight minutes"** in the Delivery row, because the page says "an eight-minute video"
  without saying whether eight is a cap. Say "eight minutes or less" if it is.
- **"Tools new to the course"** is kept as a requirement (two points in the presentation's Model
  row and two in the write-up's Model row), because the page and the deck both still require it.
  The deck's `TODO(instructor)` questions whether to keep it; if you drop it, move those four points
  to the "walked tool by tool" and "reruns without hand repair" bullets.
- **One map, not two.** The page and deck say "one professional map"; labs require two. The write-up
  Map row is a single row of ten, the sensitivity check lives in the Results row, and the second
  map is not required.
- **The write-up's Deliverables and References sections** are worth one point together (first row),
  because the page says "all the same sections" as Lab 4. I did not require a future-student rubric
  or example maps beyond the team's own map; that seemed like busywork.
- The proposal row gives two points for the meeting having happened on time with the Google
  document updated; the other eight are for what the team brought, as listed on the deck's
  "Deliverable 1" slide. I did not write a late penalty; the Late Work policy covers labs only.

### What `tools/templates/make_lab_report_template.js` would need

The rubric Markdown above uses the exact row shape the script parses (`| **Title** lead<br>• bullet (n)<br>• ... | /10 |`,
a `| **Total** | **/50** |` row), so the write-up table is readable as is. To build a final-project
template the script needs:

1. **Bounded extraction.** `extractRubric` scans the whole page for every `/10` row, so it would also
   pick up the proposal row, the four presentation rows, and the presentation-notes row (and it
   would not see the `**/40**` subtotal, which is fine). Slice the source to the write-up table
   first — from the `Lab-style write-up` heading to the next heading, or between marker comments
   such as `<!-- rubric:write-up -->` / `<!-- /rubric -->` — before applying the regex.
2. **Page and output paths.** `README` and `OUTFILE` are built from `lab-${key}`. A `final` entry
   needs explicit overrides (`docs/assignments/final-project.md` →
   `docs/assignments/final-project-report-template.docx`).
3. **A handout skeleton, not a report skeleton.** The `LABS` sections for the final project should
   be the Lab 4 page's sections (Background, Problem Statement, Spatial Considerations, Data,
   ModelBuilder Tools, Example Model, Step-by-Step Solution, Deliverables, References, Example Maps,
   Rubric), each hinted with the matching write-up bullet; the Data section seeded with the
   feasibility table columns; the Results seeded with a check-value table.
4. **Title block and checklist.** The template's title block and its "Before you submit" box assume a
   single author and a peer reviewer with a stamp. The final project needs team members, the three
   notes, and no reviewer line (the page does not require peer review of the write-up; say so if you
   want it).
5. The regex accepts `**/50**` as the only total, so the write-up's `/50` needs no change; if you
   would rather the template show the whole 100, add `\*\*\/100\*\*` to the pattern and a
   section-aware `isTotal`.
