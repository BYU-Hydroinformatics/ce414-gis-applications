# Grading

## How the grade is computed

Total points earned divided by total points possible gives your percentage, which is read against
the scale below. **No curve is used.** The number of points available for assignments, quizzes,
and exams may change slightly during the semester as the course is adjusted, for example if a pop
quiz is dropped or an in-class activity is added, so do not plan your effort around a point total
computed on the first day.

## Grading scale

| Grade | Minimum | Grade | Minimum |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | 93 % | C+ | 77 % |
| A− | 90 % | C | 73 % |
| B+ | 87 % | C− | 70 % |
| B | 83 % | D+ | 67 % |
| B− | 80 % | D | 63 % |
| | | D− | 60 % |

Below 60 % is an E.

## What is graded

Point values are from the Learning Suite assignments view (September 8, 2026) plus Lab 11 (Network Analysis), added
October 10, 2026, less Quiz 11, dropped October 26, 2026. On November 14, 2026 the final project and the
presentation notes became Lab 12 (Choose Your Own Adventure), worth 100 points, and a ninth in-class activity (Network Analysis, Week 12) was added: 1,240 points in all, matching the Learning Suite total. The grade is points-based, so the percentages below are simply each component's share of the
total and will shift a little if points are added or dropped during the semester.

| Component | Points | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Labs 1 to 11 | 50 each, 550 total | One PDF report each, on Learning Suite. Due Saturday 11:59 pm of the week assigned. |
| Lab 12 — [Choose Your Own Adventure](../assignments/lab-12/README.md) | 100 | Your own project, done individually: a five-minute training video on YouTube, a report and an ArcGIS Pro project package. Due Thursday 11:59 pm of Week 15. Labs 1 to 12 together are 650 points, about 52 %. |
| Reading quizzes (10) | 20 each, 200 total (about 16 %) | Open book, on Learning Suite, done independently. Due Saturday 11:59 pm of the week assigned. |
| Midterms 1 and 2 | 100 each | Closed book, concept based, in the Testing Center. Open Tuesday 8:00 am to Thursday 9:00 pm of Weeks 8 and 14. |
| Final exam | 100 | One question done in ArcGIS Pro in the lab during finals week; open book and open computer; three-hour block. |
| In-class activities (9) | 5 each, 45 total | Uploaded to Learning Suite the day they happen. Only presented in class. |
| Attendance | 25 | Check in on Learning Suite during each class session; each class day is an equal share of the 25 points. |
| Peer review | 20 | Review and stamp at least ten classmates' lab reports, two points each, and turn in a memo at the end listing whom you reviewed. |

The three exams together are about 24 % of the grade. Attendance, the in-class activities, and peer
review together are the **Attendance and Participation** category, about 7 %.

## Attendance

Regular attendance in class and lab sessions is critical. Some quiz and exam material is not in
the book and is only presented in class, and the in-class activities are where the concepts get
locked in.

Check in on the Learning Suite **Class Attendance** item during each class session. Days when class is canceled are marked excused for
everyone and count as attended, so your Learning Suite grade shows your attendance points as the
semester goes rather than only at the end.

## Late work policy

Lab assignments may be submitted late with a penalty of 10 % for each week late.

The same rule lets you revise a lab. If you submit on time and score below 90 %, you may fix the
problems and resubmit. The resubmission is graded as late work, so a corrected lab resubmitted
within a week of the due date can raise your score to as much as 90 %.

## AI grading disclaimer

Following a College of Engineering teaching luncheon that encouraged faculty to use AI tools to
help assess student work, I use two kinds of help when grading:

- **Grading code.** For labs with numeric results, I wrote Python code (with AI assistance) that
  checks your values against an answer computed from your own inputs. It gives the same result
  every time.
- **AI-assisted review.** For written reports and maps, an AI tool reads your work against the
  rubric and drafts a score and feedback.

I review every score and comment myself before it is entered, and I make the final call. I do
this to give you better feedback, not to replace my own judgment.
